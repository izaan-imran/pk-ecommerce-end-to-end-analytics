Business Problem & HypothesisThe Challenge: Despite strong order acquisition volumes, nearly half of all orders fail to convert into realised revenue—and because high-ticket items fail disproportionately, over two-thirds of gross revenue is lost. Management needs to identify high-risk segments and prioritise interventions.Initial Hypothesis vs. Empirical Reality: The conventional assumption in Pakistani e-commerce is that Cash on Delivery (COD) drives the highest failure rates. However, empirical analysis revealed two completely distinct failure modes:Prepaid orders suffer an alarming 63.0% failure rate, driven almost entirely by pre-fulfillment cancellations (59.1%).COD orders have a much lower overall failure rate (27.8%), failing primarily via post-delivery refunds (20.1%)—over 5x higher than prepaid refunds.3. Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering (Python)Notebook: python/01_cleaning_and_eda.ipynbData Preparation PipelineStructural Cleanup: Dropped unnamed blank columns, completely empty rows, and exact duplicate records.Schema Standardisation: Converted column headers to snake_case, renamed increment_id to order_id, and cast created_at to datetime and monetary/quantity columns to numeric types.Text Normalisation: Trimmed whitespace, lowercased strings, and replaced dirty placeholders (\N, "", "nan") with true NaN values across status, payment_method, and category_name_1.Domain Grouping: Consolidated granular statuses and payment gateways into analytical buckets:Status Mapping: complete, received $\rightarrow$ Completed | canceled $\rightarrow$ Cancelled | order_refunded, refund, closed (494 rows) $\rightarrow$ Refunded | pending, processing, holded, etc. $\rightarrow$ Other (non-final).Payment Mapping: cod, cashatdoorstep $\rightarrow$ COD (266,277 lines) | payaxis, easypay, bankalfalah, jazzwallet, apg, etc. $\rightarrow$ Prepaid (300,372 lines) | customercredit, marketingexpense, etc. $\rightarrow$ Store Credit (7,632 lines, excluded from COD vs. Prepaid comparisons).Outlier Treatment: Standard IQR filtering flagged legitimate high-value electronics (smartphones/laptops). To preserve genuine business transactions, only the extreme top 0.1% (above the 99.9th percentile of qty_ordered or item_total) was removed.Feature Engineering: Created item_total (price * qty_ordered), binary outcome flags (is_cancelled, is_refunded, is_failed, is_final), quartile-based value_band (Low, Medium, High, Premium), discount_band, and net_revenue.7. SQL Analytical Framework (MySQL)Script: sql/analysis_queries.sql | Database: pk_ecommerce | Table: orders_cleanThe SQL layer answers 17 structured business questions using Common Table Expressions (CTEs), Window Functions (SUM() OVER(), LAG()), conditional aggregations, and multi-dimensional risk segmentation:Data Integrity & Volume Trends (Queries 0–2): Row count validation, monthly volume/revenue trajectories, and overall status share using window functions.Payment Mechanics (Queries 3, 3A–3C): COD vs. Prepaid failure comparisons, gateway-level cancellation rankings, YoY consistency checks, and category cross-tabulations.Category & Pricing Sensitivity (Queries 4–6B): Category failure thresholds (HAVING), discount-band failure analysis (CASE + FIELD() sorting), and quartile order-value risk tiers.Anomaly Detection & Customer Retention (Queries 7–10, Bonus): Month-over-month failure spikes using LAG(), category revenue leakage ranking, repeat buyer share via dual CTEs, high-risk COD segment isolation using scalar subqueries, and global revenue leakage calculation.Sample Analytical Query (Payment Type Comparison)SELECT 
    payment_type,
    COUNT(*) AS final_orders,
    ROUND(100 * AVG(is_cancelled), 2) AS cancel_rate_pct,
    ROUND(100 * AVG(is_refunded), 2) AS refund_rate_pct,
    ROUND(100 * AVG(is_failed), 2) AS failed_rate_pct
FROM orders_clean
WHERE is_final = 1
  AND payment_type <> 'Store Credit'
GROUP BY payment_type
ORDER BY failed_rate_pct DESC;

The three largest gateways—payaxis (62.8%), easypay (62.9%), and bankalfalah (71.0%)—account for ~200,900 order lines with massive pre-fulfillment drop-offs, while apg reached 78.3% and mygateway 97.6%.YoY COD Deterioration: COD failure rates climbed steadily from 25.0% (2016) to 28.3% (2017) to 30.6% (2018), while overall platform failure rose from 42% to 52% as prepaid order share grew from 41% to 66%.9.2 High-Ticket COD Risk MultiplierCOD failure risk scales 2.7x with order value: jumping from 19.0% on Low value orders to 24.4% (Medium), 34.7% (High), and 51.8% on Premium COD orders. Combined High & Premium COD segments fail at 40.27% vs. 21.65% for lower-tier COD orders.9.3 Category Concentration & Fashion Returns5 Categories Exceed 50% Failure: Others (68.3%), Mobiles & Tablets (56.6%), Entertainment (56.4%), Computing (54.8%), and Appliances (51.5%).85% of Financial Loss in Top 3 Categories: Mobiles & Tablets alone loses Rs 1,676M (~60% of total lost revenue), followed by Appliances (Rs 378M) and Entertainment (Rs 317M).Post-Delivery Fashion Returns: Unlike electronics (which fail via cancellation), Men's Fashion (18.55% refunded) and Women's Fashion (15.79% refunded) suffer heavily from post-delivery returns.9.4 Seasonality & Discount PatternsNovember Peak Resilience: November sales volumes surged 3x–5.8x above monthly averages (68.7K in Nov 2016; 82.4K in Nov 2017), yet failure rates remained steady at 43.4% and 45.4%—demonstrating operational stability during peak load.September 2017 Anomaly: Order volume dropped sharply to 8,201 lines while failure spiked to 56.7%, indicating a localized technical or operational disruption.Full-Price Prepaid Drop-off: Full-price (No discount) prepaid orders failed at 80.26%, compared to 44.9%–63.1% across discounted tiers.10. Strategic RecommendationsOverhaul Prepaid Checkout & Payment Gateways: Conduct an immediate technical audit of payment timeouts, retry logic, and gateway failure logs on payaxis, easypay, and bankalfalah (~200K order lines).Pre-Dispatch Verification for High-Value COD: Implement automated WhatsApp/IVR confirmation workflows for High and Premium COD orders (40.3% failure rate) prior to courier dispatch.Hold Off on Forcing Prepaid for High-Ticket Buyers: Because Premium prepaid orders also fail at 64.2%, fix gateway reliability first before testing small partial advance deposits.Curb Fashion & COD Return Rates: Deploy interactive sizing guides, high-resolution product visuals, transparent return policies, and mandatory return reason capture to reduce COD (20.1%) and Fashion (15.8%–18.6%) refunds.Root-Cause Audit on Top Loss Categories: Review real-time inventory sync, pricing accuracy, and courier SLAs for Mobiles & Tablets, Appliances, and Entertainment (~85% of revenue lost).Instrument Mandatory Failure Reason Codes: Capture structured cancellation and refund reasons at checkout/return, monitor monthly failure KPIs by payment type, and audit the September 2017 spike.11. Methodology LimitationsInferred Root Causes: The raw dataset does not include explicit cancellation or refund reason codes; operational causes are inferred from transactional patterns.Order-Line Granularity: All counts and rates reflect individual line items (order_line) rather than consolidated multi-item baskets.Status & Store Credit Handling: closed (494 rows) was mapped to Refunded; non-final statuses (pending, processing, holded) and internal Store Credit adjustments were excluded from COD vs. Prepaid failure comparisons.Discount Attribution: Unapplied promo codes on abandoned/cancelled checkouts may influence observed discount-band failure rates.13.
